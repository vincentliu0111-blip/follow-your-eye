TRUST YOUR EYE

Which painting catches your eye?

Art preference starts with a choice you can make without knowing an artist’s name. In Follow Your Eye, look at two paintings, choose the one you prefer, and see where your choices overlap with precomputed AI judgments after a round.

How to compare two paintings

First, look at each whole image before concentrating on a detail. Notice where your attention lands, then ask what makes you want to return. You might respond to a subject, a color relationship, a patch of light or an arrangement of shapes. You do not need to justify a preference before making it.

If you feel stuck, compare one thing at a time: open space versus dense detail, a strong focal point versus a wandering view, or long lines versus broken marks. These are ways to look, not rules for declaring a winner.

A preference is not an art-history quiz

Knowing a work’s context can change what you notice, but this experience does not grade that knowledge. A famous painting is not automatically the choice you have to make. Skipping is useful when neither work holds your attention or you do not want to choose.

If you start from a work’s guide page, you have already spent time with that painting. Keep that familiarity in mind when thinking about your response to the first comparison.

What does comparing your taste with AI mean?

After a round, the site compares your choices with previously computed AI-judge preferences and a separate image-feature model. Agreement means the same painting was selected in those particular comparisons. The AI is not a referee deciding whether your taste is correct.

A short round cannot establish a complete taste profile. Different paintings, familiarity, screen size and the moment you are looking may all matter. The results describe this round; they are not a psychological diagnosis or an objective beauty score.

What happens if a choice cannot save online?

You can keep choosing and see the locally calculated round result. Waiting choices can upload when the connection returns while the site is open. Browser storage can fail or be cleared, so unsynced progress is not guaranteed to survive; the page warns when progress is only in the current tab.

Public vote counts include accepted online saves, not every local choice. The connection-check page tests read access without submitting a choice.

Questions about art preference comparisons

Do I need to know art to participate?

No. Choose what attracts you, take time to look, or skip. The experience compares preferences rather than testing art knowledge.

Does the AI judge my taste while I play?

No. The painting preferences used for comparison were computed earlier. Your result shows overlap with those saved preferences, not a live judgment of you.

Can I start with a particular painting?

Yes. The focused artwork guides link to an existing comparison containing that work. If you have already made a choice in a current round, it stays intact and the requested work starts the next round.
