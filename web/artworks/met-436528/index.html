LOOK CLOSELY

Van Gogh’s Irises (1890): looking at color and line

The observations and questions below are Follow Your Eye editorial, based on the image; they are not museum commentary.

A bouquet, rather than a garden

This image shows flowers gathered in a pale vase on a green surface. Start with the whole silhouette. The leaves reach outward while many flower heads collect near the center. Does your attention stay with the cluster, or follow the stems toward the edges?

Compare two kinds of line

Trace one long leaf, then the edge of a folded petal. The leaf gives you a sweeping direction; the petal breaks into shorter turns. Try looking at those shapes before naming the flower. Which movement makes you want to keep looking?

Give the background a turn

The pale field around the bouquet has visible brush marks of its own. Compare its open space with the packed leaves and blossoms. You can prefer that contrast without deciding that every dense or colorful painting will suit you.

What should I notice in Van Gogh’s Irises?

Begin with the spreading leaves, the clustered flower heads and the pale space around them. These are looking exercises, not a test of the artist’s intention.
