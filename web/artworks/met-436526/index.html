LOOK CLOSELY

Van Gogh’s First Steps, after Millet: how gesture connects a scene

The observations and questions below are Follow Your Eye editorial, based on the image; they are not museum commentary.

Begin with the reaching hands

At the left, a crouching adult extends both arms toward the child at the right. Follow that gesture across the open ground. The space between the figures is part of the scene: it gives your eye a distance to travel.

Find the figures in the garden

Blue clothing appears against a largely green and yellow setting. Look at the adult and child on the right, then the figure on the left. Which contrast makes a person easiest to find? Compare that with the quieter shapes behind them.

Move between story and surface

You can read the picture as a small human event, or pause over its short marks and patches of color. Try both approaches. If another painting attracts you more, ask whether you preferred its subject, its arrangement, or simply the way it held your attention.

Why does the title say “after Millet”?

It identifies the relationship to Millet’s composition. The Met explains that Van Gogh worked from a photograph and interpreted the source in color.
